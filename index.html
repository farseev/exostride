The short answer

ExoStride US-H-1.0Pro is a 2.4 kg powered hip exoskeleton sold in Singapore by HT Science & Development Pte. Ltd. It senses your gait and delivers up to 16 Nm of adaptive assistance at the hip, removing up to 80% of the lower-body load per step, cutting climbing energy by up to 35% and daily commute exertion by 30–50%. It runs up to 4 hours per 2-hour charge, is IP54-rated for rain and dust, folds, and starts on one button with no app. It is worn over clothing and used at home, on trails or on the commute without a prescription.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is ExoStride and who makes it?
ExoStride is a powered hip exoskeleton made by HT Science. The model sold in Singapore is the US-H-1.0Pro, distributed exclusively by HT Science & Development Pte. Ltd. (UEN 202033252G001).
How much does an ExoStride cost in Singapore?
Pricing is quoted directly by the Singapore team, since it depends on configuration and fitting. Message +65 9122 7009 on WhatsApp or call +65 8355 0979 for a current figure.
Is ExoStride a medical device?
No. It is a consumer mobility-assist exoskeleton used for rehabilitation support, outdoor activity and everyday walking. It is worn at home without a prescription and does not diagnose or treat any condition.
How heavy is it and does it need an app?
2.4 kg, foldable, and one-button — no app or setup is needed to simply walk.
How long does the battery last?
Up to 4 hours of assistance on a 2-hour full charge. Stairs and steep gradients draw more than flat ground.
Can I use it in the rain?
Yes. It is IP54-rated — dust-protected and resistant to splashing water from any direction, which covers typical Singapore rain. Avoid full submersion.
Where can I try ExoStride in Singapore?
Fittings are done on your own route — at home, on a trail such as Bukit Timah or MacRitchie, or on your actual commute. Book via the form on this page or WhatsApp +65 9122 7009.
Who is ExoStride for?
Three groups: people in rehabilitation or with reduced mobility, hikers and outdoor enthusiasts, and urban commuters who walk stair-heavy routes daily.
